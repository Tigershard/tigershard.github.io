Stripes privacy policy

Last updated: 7 November 2026

Stripes is a personal Android app for organizing a vinyl record collection. This page explains what it does with your information. In short: your data stays on your phone and in your own accounts, and the developer never receives it.

What Stripes stores

  • Your records and wishlist: what you type in or add from Discogs, such as artist, title, condition, price and notes. They're stored in a database on your phone.
  • Your Discogs username and personal access token, if you connect Discogs. The token is stored encrypted on your phone and is left out of phone backups.
  • Which Google account is connected (its email address, for display in Settings) and the ID of the spreadsheet Stripes created.
  • Saved cover images from Discogs, so they show offline.

Camera

The barcode scanner uses the camera only while the scanner screen is open. Barcodes are read on the phone. No pictures are saved or sent anywhere: only the barcode's numbers are used, to look the record up on Discogs.

Google account and Google Drive

If you connect a Google account, Stripes asks for one permission: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file. That lets it create one spreadsheet, "Vinyl Organizer", in your Drive, and read and change that spreadsheet and any other file Stripes itself created. It can't see any other file in your Drive.

Stripes uses this access only to keep that spreadsheet in step with your collection on the phone. It also reads your account's email address from Google Drive, to show which account is connected. The data goes directly between your phone and Google. It isn't sent to the developer or anyone else, used for advertising, or sold.

Stripes' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Discogs

When you search, scan or import, Stripes sends your search text, barcode or catalogue number to Discogs, along with your Discogs token, to get the results. Discogs' own privacy policy, linked at the bottom of discogs.com, covers what Discogs does with requests.

What Stripes doesn't do

  • No analytics, tracking or ads.
  • No account with the developer, and no server of the developer's.
  • Nothing is shared with or sold to anyone.

Removing your data

  • Disconnect Google or Discogs in Stripes' Settings to forget those accounts on the phone.
  • To take away Stripes' Google access completely, remove it in your Google Account under Security, "Your connections to third-party apps & services".
  • Uninstalling Stripes deletes everything it stored on the phone. The spreadsheet stays in your Drive until you delete it.
